# Key messages

## In one line

A shop owner types one sentence, and Brightpost returns ready-to-post, on-brand campaign images and post text for six platforms.

## In one paragraph

Brightpost is a chat app that makes marketing posts for independent shops (a fictional app, the worked example here). The shop owner describes the post they need in their own words, for example "First frost is forecast this weekend. Winter commuting posts for the Ridgeline 7." The app answers with three campaign options built only from the brand hub: hub photos, Brightpost logos and brand colours, plus the shop's own logo. Every image is checked before the shop owner sees it. The shop owner can edit any word, ask for changes in plain words, add platforms, switch language, then download the kit and post. The app remembers the shop's rules and design choices, so the next campaign starts from them.

## Proof points, with the moment in the film

Times are minutes:seconds into the walkthrough film. Both versions (captions and voiceover) have the same picture, so the times apply to both.

1. **One sentence in, three options out.** The shop owner types one sentence at 0:22. Three different options appear at 0:29, each built from a different hub photo. The app's own timer, "Worked for 56 s", is shown at about 0:37.
2. **Only brand-hub material, checked before it is shown.** "Three on-brand options from the brand hub" (0:29) and "Every image is checked before you see it" (0:32). The app checks crops, contrast, safe areas and logos, and an AI reviewer looks at each option before it appears.
3. **Every word can be edited, even on the image.** At 0:53 the shop owner clicks the headline on the picture and rewrites it. No new AI request is needed for that.
4. **Changes in plain words.** At 1:02 the shop owner asks: "Try a new design: photo on top, text in a teal block underneath." The new layout arrives at 1:13 ("Worked for 34 s"), with the same photo, words and brand rules.
5. **Six platforms, 18 formats, no waiting.** At 1:18 the shop owner adds the other platforms from a menu, with no AI wait. From 1:21 the film tours all 18 formats: Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, LinkedIn, display ads and newsletter.
6. **It speaks the shop's language.** The app itself runs in 8 languages (1:35). At 1:43 the shop owner asks for the post in German; from 1:50 the image and the post text are both in German.
7. **Tell it once, and it remembers.** At 2:00 the shop owner gives a standing rule (a sign-off line, and "closed on Sundays"); at 2:09 they set their own brand kit (colour, button shape, logo order). The next campaign follows both: shop logo first, teal style, pill button (2:40) and the sign-off added without asking (2:45). The whole kit downloads in one click at 2:51.

## Words to use and to avoid

- Say "options", "creatives" or "posts"; say "the brand hub" for the source material.
- Say "checked" or "reviewed", not "guaranteed" or "approved". The app checks each image; a person should still look before publishing (the app says so under the chat box: "Brightpost can make mistakes. Check before you publish.").
- The shop in the film, Harbor Bikes, is fictional. Do not present it as a real customer.
